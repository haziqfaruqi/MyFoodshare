### 📖 Biography

**Background:**
Marcus grew up in a family of food vendors in Penang and learned to cook from his grandmother. After completing his culinary diploma, he worked in several high-end hotels in Kuala Lumpur for 10 years before opening his own restaurant, "Flavors of Asia," in 2015. His restaurant specializes in authentic Malaysian dishes with a modern twist.

**Current Situation:**
Marcus runs a bustling restaurant in a busy commercial area of KL. His establishment serves approximately 80-120 customers daily, with higher volumes during weekends and public holidays. Due to the unpredictable nature of walk-in customers and the need to maintain fresh ingredients, his kitchen often prepares excess food that goes unsold by closing time.

**Daily Routine:**
- **5:30 AM**: Arrives at the restaurant, checks inventory, meets with suppliers
- **7:00 AM**: Supervises food preparation with kitchen staff (5 cooks)
- **11:00 AM - 3:00 PM**: Lunch service rush
- **3:00 PM - 5:00 PM**: Short break, handles admin work, checks social media
- **5:00 PM - 10:00 PM**: Dinner service
- **10:00 PM - 11:00 PM**: Closing, inventory check, sees leftover food

**Pain Point:**
Every night, Marcus sees 3-5kg of perfectly good food that didn't sell – curries that were prepared in large batches, rice dishes, side dishes, and desserts. It breaks his heart to throw away food that took hours to prepare, especially knowing there are people who could benefit from it.

**Values:**
- Deeply values his grandmother's teaching: "Never waste food"
- Committed to reducing environmental impact
- Believes in community support and giving back
- Takes pride in food quality and safety

### 😤 Frustrations

#### 1. **Food Waste Guilt**
> *"Every night I throw away 3-5 kilograms of perfectly good food. It's expensive, wasteful, and it bothers me morally. My grandmother would be disappointed."*

- Throws away RM 150-250 worth of food weekly (RM 600-1000/month)
- Feels guilty about environmental impact
- Worries about contributing to landfill problems
- Knows the food is still safe and nutritious

#### 2. **Unpredictable Demand**
> *"I never know if we'll have 50 or 150 customers. I have to prepare enough, but that means leftovers when it's a slow day."*

- Cannot accurately predict daily customer volume
- Must prepare in batches for efficiency
- Weather, events, and holidays affect traffic unpredictably
- Preparing too little = lost sales; preparing too much = waste

#### 3. **No Efficient Disposal Solution**
> *"I've tried calling NGOs before, but by the time they respond, the food's already cold. I need something faster and more reliable."*

- Manual calling of NGOs is time-consuming
- NGOs often can't pick up on short notice
- Coordination happens too late in the evening
- No systematic way to track donations

#### 4. **Time Constraints**
> *"By 10 PM, I'm exhausted. I don't have time to make phone calls and wait for pickups. I need something quick and simple."*

- Works 16-hour days regularly
- Too tired to coordinate donations manually
- Needs solution that doesn't add to workload
- Limited staff available after closing time

#### 5. **Trust and Safety Concerns**
> *"I worry – what if someone gets sick from my food? How do I know it reached the right people? I need proper documentation."*

- Concerned about food safety liability
- Wants verification that food was properly received
- Needs proof for tax purposes (if applicable)
- Worries about reputation if something goes wrong

#### 6. **Administrative Burden**
> *"I run a restaurant, not a charity. I don't have time for paperwork, but I want to do good and maybe get some recognition for it."*

- Already overwhelmed with restaurant paperwork
- Doesn't want complex donation processes
- Needs simple, streamlined system
- Wants impact tracking without extra effort

### 🎨 User Scenario

**Scenario: Friday Night, 10:15 PM**

Marcus finishes closing procedures and notices 4 containers of leftover curry (enough for 20 servings) and 2 trays of desserts.

1. **10:15 PM**: Opens MyFoodshare app → Taps "Donate Food"
2. **10:16 PM**: Takes photos of containers → App auto-suggests "Curry" and "Desserts"
3. **10:17 PM**: Adjusts quantity to "20 servings" → Sets expiry "Tonight, 11:30 PM"
4. **10:18 PM**: Taps "Post Listing" → Listing submitted
5. **10:19 PM**: Receives notification: "3 NGOs are interested!"
6. **10:20 PM**: Reviews NGO profiles → Approves "Hope Foundation" (4.8 stars, 50 past pickups)
7. **10:21 PM**: App generates QR code → Marcus screenshots it
8. **10:45 PM**: Receives notification: "Hope Foundation is on the way"
9. **11:00 PM**: NGO volunteer arrives → Scans QR code → Marcus gets "Verified" notification
10. **11:05 PM**: Food handed over → Marcus locks up and leaves
11. **Next morning**: Receives notification: "Pickup completed! 20 meals helped 15 families. View your impact."

**Total time spent**: 6 minutes
**Food saved**: 20 meals
**Feeling**: Satisfied, relieved, proud

### 📖 Biography

**Background:**
Siti grew up in a lower-income family in Kelantan and witnessed food insecurity firsthand. After completing her social work degree on scholarship, she moved to Kuala Lumpur and joined Hope Foundation, an NGO dedicated to fighting hunger and poverty. She started as a volunteer and worked her way up to Program Coordinator.

**Current Situation:**
Siti manages Hope Foundation's food distribution program, which serves 200+ low-income families across three communities in KL. The organization operates on a tight budget with limited resources. They distribute food parcels every week and run a community kitchen that prepares meals for homeless individuals.

**Daily Routine:**
- **7:00 AM**: Checks food inventory at NGO center
- **8:00 AM**: Coordinates with volunteers for morning meal prep
- **9:00 AM - 12:00 PM**: Community kitchen operations (breakfast/lunch for 50 people)
- **12:00 PM - 2:00 PM**: Administrative work, responds to donor emails
- **2:00 PM - 5:00 PM**: Home visits to beneficiary families, assessments
- **5:00 PM - 7:00 PM**: Coordinates evening food distribution
- **7:00 PM - 8:00 PM**: Plans next day's activities, checks donations

**Pain Point:**
Hope Foundation struggles with inconsistent food donations. Some days they have excess supplies, other days they barely have enough to feed everyone. The unpredictability makes planning difficult, and they often have to decline fresh food donations if they're too far away or require immediate pickup.

**Values:**
- Deeply committed to reducing hunger and poverty
- Believes no one should go to bed hungry
- Values efficiency and minimizing waste
- Treats beneficiaries with dignity and respect
- Passionate about community empowerment

---

### 😤 Frustrations

#### 1. **Unpredictable Food Supply**
> *"Some weeks we have too much, some weeks not enough. I can't plan meal distributions when I don't know what food we'll have tomorrow."*

- Donation quantities vary wildly week to week
- Cannot promise consistent meals to beneficiaries
- Have to turn away families when supplies run low
- Difficult to budget and plan menus

#### 2. **Limited Geographic Coverage**
> *"There are probably donors 10 km away with food, but we don't know about them. We only get donations from restaurants we have personal relationships with."*

- Only aware of donors they know personally
- Miss out on potential donations in neighboring areas
- Limited volunteer transportation capacity (1 van)
- Cannot efficiently plan multi-pickup routes

#### 3. **Last-Minute Coordination**
> *"Restaurants call at 10 PM saying they have food. By the time we mobilize volunteers and drive there, it's 11 PM and the food is cold or the restaurant is already closed."*

- Restaurants notify too late (closing time)
- Volunteers unavailable on short notice
- Traffic delays make timing unpredictable
- Food quality degrades during delays

#### 4. **Manual Communication Inefficiency**
> *"I spend 2-3 hours daily on the phone and WhatsApp coordinating pickups. That's time I could spend actually helping people."*

- Endless phone calls and text messages
- No centralized system for tracking donations
- Difficult to coordinate multiple pickups simultaneously
- Communication gaps lead to missed pickups

#### 5. **Lack of Visibility on Available Food**
> *"I wish I could see what food is available in real-time. Instead, I find out through random calls or messages, often when it's too late."*

- No systematic way to browse available donations
- Cannot search for specific food types needed
- Miss time-sensitive opportunities
- Reactive instead of proactive

#### 6. **Quality and Safety Uncertainty**
> *"Sometimes we pick up food and find it's not as described, or already past its best quality. We need clear information upfront."*

- Cannot see food photos before committing
- Unclear about quantities and expiry times
- Worry about food safety for beneficiaries
- Need better documentation for liability

#### 7. **No Accountability or Tracking**
> *"We don't have proper records of donations. When grant applications ask 'How much food did you distribute?' I have to estimate. That's not professional."*

- Manual record-keeping is incomplete
- Cannot generate impact reports easily
- Difficult to prove organizational effectiveness
- Miss opportunities for grants due to lack of data

#### 8. **Volunteer Coordination Challenges**
> *"I need to know WHERE pickups are, WHEN they're available, and WHO can go. Right now it's chaos trying to match volunteers with pickups."*

- Cannot efficiently assign volunteers based on location
- Volunteers miss pickup opportunities
- Overlap or gaps in coverage
- Poor route optimization

## Design Implications

### For Restaurant Owner (Marcus):

**UI/UX Priorities:**
1. **⚡ Speed First**: Maximum 3 taps to post listing
2. **📱 Big Buttons**: Finger-friendly (tired hands at closing time)
3. **📸 Photo-Centric**: Less typing, more photos
4. **🔔 Unobtrusive Notifications**: Not during lunch/dinner rush (11 AM - 2 PM, 6 PM - 9 PM)
5. **📊 Visual Impact**: Charts and numbers, not text reports

**Feature Priority:**
- P0: Quick listing, QR generation, impact dashboard
- P1: Match approval, scheduled donations
- P2: Social sharing, badges

---

### For NGO Coordinator (Siti):

**UI/UX Priorities:**
1. **🗺️ Map First**: Default view is map with food locations
2. **🔍 Powerful Filtering**: Quick filters always visible
3. **📄 Information Dense**: Show all details without extra taps
4. **⚡ Fast Response**: One-tap interest expression
5. **📊 Data Export**: Easy report generation

**Feature Priority:**
- P0: Map discovery, real-time notifications, QR scanner, impact tracking
- P1: Volunteer assignment, route optimization
- P2: Beneficiary tracking, social sharing
